# Why AirDay counts kilometres

A shared school run sounds efficient until the second pickup adds a long detour. AirDay asks for both the usual driving distance and the proposed route. Two separate four-kilometre trips total eight kilometres. A nine-kilometre shared route would add a kilometre. The result changes sign in the interface, so a family can reject a poor swap.

Air quality and driving impact have different evidence behind them. The air panel identifies the city estimate, provider and timestamp. The trip panel uses household inputs. Google Antigravity helped shape the instructions for Gemini Nano to explain these facts in everyday language and preserve uncertainty when a reading is unavailable.

A family can compare school, shopping and community journeys, then choose a small indoor change. The design keeps the plan available even on a phone without a local language model. A next iteration could compare forecasts at different pickup times.
